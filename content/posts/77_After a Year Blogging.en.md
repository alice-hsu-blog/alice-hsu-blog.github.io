My blog turned one last week! All through late September I kept thinking about how to celebrate. At first I considered hanging a big red banner on the homepage (thank goodness I didn’t). In the end, I decided to overhaul the site into something that feels more like me. Here are the most noticeable changes:

**1. A redesigned theme**

For the site’s first year, I used the Hugo Narrow theme and adjusted its styles to suit my needs (many thanks to tom2almighty, the creator of [Hugo Narrow](https://themes.gohugo.io/themes/hugo-narrow/)!). As time went on, I noticed more and more of a gap between the site I imagined and the original theme, so I used the anniversary as a chance to rebuild it with a more minimalist look. I chose a burgundy that matches the way I dress, along with two fonts I love, Noto Serif TC and Newsreader.

The homepage is redesigned too, with a very cool line-drawn portrait that redraws itself when you click it. And it looks so good in both the dark and light themes that I can’t pick a favorite.

![](/images/Post/77/1.webp)

**2. A new “Prayer” page**

Following [my earlier post](/posts/2026/09/someone-is-praying-for-you/), prayer has officially become a long-term project on this site, so I gave it its own page to make it easier for readers to find and fill out the form.

![](/images/Post/77/8.png)

**3. Three new features on the “Posts” page**

The “Posts” page used to be a simple chronological list. As the number of posts grew, I added three sections: featured posts, year buttons, and a random button, which I hope will make it easier for readers to find something they want to read.

![](/images/Post/77/9.png)

**4. Post navigation can now switch between date and category**

The “Previous” and “Next” buttons at the bottom of each post can now switch to browsing within the same category, perfect for readers who like to binge a whole category in one go.

![](/images/Post/77/10.png)

**5. BlogBlog Club Party posts now have a tag**

Readers can now click the “BlogBlog Club Party" label beneath the title of a submitted post to find everything I’ve contributed. Since the party is the only thing using tags at the moment, I’m leaving tags out of the main menu for now.

![](/images/Post/77/11.png)

---

Of all the changes in this big overhaul, one of my favorites is also completely useless: in my three “Poetry” posts, I [removed the background from the photos of my handwritten manuscripts and made them invert in dark mode!](/zh-tw/posts/2026/09/cannot-sleep-at-night/) (in Chinese) Now, in either the light or dark theme, it looks as if I wrote directly on the website. I just adore it so much!

![](/images/Post/77/7.png)

---

When the blog turned six months old, I wrote [a post](/posts/2026/07/after-half-year-blogging/) about what I’d learned. Another six months have passed, and apart from adding my *Dream of the Red Chamber* series and the Echo project, my thoughts are mostly the same. If there’s anything more to say, it’s this: writing is a marvelous thing. It is a “second completion” of life. An event that might otherwise have been hastily filed away in my personal memory bank gets sorted, chewed over, and pruned until it becomes something singular and glowing. And, cliché as it sounds, writing really can change you. It can clarify thoughts that were once hazy ([What else can sadness be?](/posts/2026/09/the-truth-of-sadness/)), complete a long-awaited project ([finishing *Dream of the Red Chamber* and writing about it](/zh-tw/posts/2026/09/read-dream-of-the-red-chamber-101-to-120-chapters/), in Chinese), and reframe a situation that seems awful ([Can’t sleep at night](/zh-tw/posts/2026/09/cannot-sleep-at-night/).) Writing is also a second completion of the self.

What else can writing be? What else can I become? I hope I can stay curious and keep writing, happily, for my own delight.
